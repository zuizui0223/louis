# Ecological publication allocation — 25 October 2026

**Decision for author review:** treat the new California day/night category-succession analysis as the **original ecological research manuscript candidate**, and treat the longer ODSP–batter–Louisiana comparison as a **separate conceptual/ecological synthesis draft**, not as four new independent empirical validations.

## Principal original ecological paper

**Manuscript:** [Twilight reorganization of winter habitat use](CALIFORNIA_DIEL_HABITAT_ORIGINAL_ECOLOGY_V1.md).

- The biological response is a *successive used-habitat-category transition*, not overall movement frequency, original habitat selection strength, camera observation of foraging or a novel prediction algorithm.
- The predeclared exploratory day/night boundary contrast was positive among lesser snow geese only: **+8.47 pp** (95% candidate-ID interval +5.72 to +11.16; 69 estimable tags, 54 positive individual contrasts).
- A separate **post-result** description showed Protected-category observation followed by Rice-category observation at nightfall in lesser snow geese (**+32.36 pp**, 95% interval +22.13 to +43.33; 47 tags) and pintails (**+55.54 pp**, +37.53 to +72.77; 18 tags). Other species were uncertain or insufficient; the reverse Rice→Protected sequence showed no comparably robust dusk enhancement.
- Original sources: 380,578 records from **one California USGS study**, 329 source bird-years, 254 candidate biological tag identities, 56,591 exact-hour pairs, 9,586 cross-photoperiod and 46,947 within-period qualifying pairs. The two classes Protected and Rice refer to consecutive recorded *destinations*, not a verified geographic flight track.
- Biological question: *how does the daily order of habitat states differ among species sharing winter agricultural/wetland landscapes?* Candidate ecological interpretation: time-ordered use of managed protected areas and rice agriculture; actual refuge and feeding behaviors were not separately witnessed.
- Figures: [species-specific boundary switching](Figure3_species_diel_habitat_switching.svg) and [directed Protected/Rice category succession](Figure4_diel_protected_rice_sequences.svg).

**Explicit novelty limit and relevant previous ecology:** Overton and Casazza (2023) already established the four taxa's day/night movement rhythms and habitat functional responses from these telemetry sources; they are not rediscovered here. Diel wetland sanctuary selection in California pintail was already described by Casazza et al. (2012), and nocturnal rice feeding/daytime roosting by northern pintail was demonstrated with GPS in an Iberian rice region by Parejo et al. (2019). Therefore claiming that the authors first discovered nocturnal rice use or refuge-to-rice behavior would be false. The additional response analysed here is a **within-source sequential habitat state transition conditioned on prior category and current matched availability**, with explicit species contrasts and uncertainty.

## Cross-repository ecology synthesis remains separate

**Draft:** [Shared places, different ecological lives](SHARED_PLACES_DIFFERENT_STATES_ECOLOGY_MANUSCRIPT_V1.md).

The Serengeti species × detection time, bat repeatable vertical-use individuality, King Rail environmental-depth buffering, and California state transitions are biologically informative but **measure different endpoints**. The ODSP *Tadarida* and batter focal data overlap; the California species share one source study, and King Rail events have no authoritative geographic-step origin-to-depth join.

This manuscript may provide a perspective, integrative ecological hypothesis or future review, but should **not** currently be represented as a separately validated original multi-taxon meta-analysis. To support such a paper as original research, one would need an independent shared design or multiple previously unanalysed, comparable field datasets with a common ecological response.

## What is not the central ecological manuscript

The separate two-plot K estimation and identifiability counterexamples establish a sampling limitation. They are **supplementary methods/feasibility evidence**, not a standalone ecological conclusion about animal behavior or habitat management. The K estimator did not robustly improve King Rail held-out prediction over mean mismatch and local heterogeneity. Do not choose another K band or threshold to rescue it.

## Necessary field ecology follow-up

An independently marked waterfowl sample with full GPS move origins and destinations, direct behavioral identification near dusk/dawn, flooding/hunting/refuge management schedules, and time-aligned availability at feasible alternate destinations would test whether the observed category succession corresponds to actual roost/feeding circuits. Species-specific cross-year held-out tests and independent landscape replication should be frozen before new outcomes are read. Monitor source sunrise labels and movement-step selection biases.

## Publication quality stop list

1. Real authorship, contributors' agreement and source study animal-care/ethics details.
2. Independent novelty/overlap review against existing published source analysis and the other ODSP, batter, King Rail and California manuscripts.
3. Reference accuracy, target journal category and figure final visual review.
4. Clarify whether candidate individual prefixes have formally authenticated unique tag IDs.
5. Acknowledge hypothesis-generation: the directed Protected/Rice route contrast was planned **after** inspecting a descriptive signal. Do not claim it was confirmatory, independently replicated, or management-effective.

The original three source repositories and their ecological results are unchanged. No manuscript has been submitted to a journal.
