# Louisiana novelty audit — 2026-10-05

## Purpose

The Lake Erie King Rail result is statistically strong. This audit asks a harder question:

> **what is actually new, given that habitat choice, niche tracking and environmental-variance reduction are already established?**

The paper should be built around the narrow part that survives that comparison.

---

## 1. What is not novel

Do not claim novelty for:

- King Rail selection of shallow water;
- habitat selection reducing environmental variance in general;
- movement to environmentally similar places;
- organisms regulating the environment they experience through habitat choice;
- broad climatic / hydrological niche tracking.

Relevant prior boundaries include:

- Chesson & Yang (2019), DOI 10.3389/fevo.2019.00363 — experienced environments can differ from local environments under redistribution.
- Clark et al. (2020), DOI 10.1086/706196 — habitat/resource choice is an environmental-regulation process that can buffer environmental variation.
- Knight et al. (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.actao.2025.104103 — white-tailed deer habitat selection explicitly analysed as reduction of environmental variance relative to available habitat.
- Shoebill and little-bustard tracking studies already demonstrate movement associated with environmental-state continuity at broader spatial/seasonal scales.

Therefore:

> **"animals use habitat to reduce environmental variance" is not the Louisiana novelty.**

---

## 2. What the Lake Erie design adds

The distinctive feature is the event-matched dynamic counterfactual.

For every repeated bird event, the archive contains:

- the water depth actually used;
- nearby random habitat measured at approximately the same time.

This lets the study ask:

> **when the local available environment changes, how much of that change is transmitted into the state actually experienced by the individual?**

That differs from comparing:

- mean used versus mean available habitat;
- variance of used versus a broad study-area background;
- seasonal niches across distant ranges.

The primary dynamic result is:

- observed within-bird used-on-available water-depth slope: **0.162**;
- matched pseudo-used null median: **1.001**;
- coupling reduction: **83.8%**.

Thus local hydrological variation was only weakly expressed in the realised used state.

The paper's strongest quantitative idea is therefore **environmental transmission / coupling**, not simply variance reduction.

---

## 3. Why the result is not one common shallow-water set point

The ten birds differ substantially in the median water depth they used.

Across the exact-point buffering analysis, individual median used depths range from approximately:

- **1.6 cm** to **16.9 cm**.

The strength of hydrological coupling reduction is not clearly ranked by the median used state in this n=10 panel.

This is a post-hoc descriptive boundary, not a new primary test.

It nevertheless argues against a simplistic interpretation in which every bird merely converges on one common fixed water-depth target.

A more biologically plausible interpretation is:

> **individuals can buffer different realised hydrological states against temporal changes in their own local availability.**

Do not call this individual specialization without an independent repeatability/design test.

---

## 4. The strongest current contribution

Use:

> **Resident King Rails strongly reduce the transmission of local hydrological variation into the environmental state they actually use, measured against event-matched local availability.**

This is more precise than:

> King Rails reduce environmental variance.

The distinction matters because the transmission formulation asks about an environmental perturbation and its realised exposure, not only about the width of a habitat distribution.

---

## 5. The next general question

The current data also show that buffering is finite.

During each bird's most unusual local hydrological conditions:

- 9/10 birds retain positive extreme-condition buffering;
- one individual shows negative retention;
- the magnitude varies strongly among birds.

The next general ecological hypothesis is therefore:

> **Animals remain resident while suitable environmental states remain reachable within a familiar local portfolio; when local compensation fails, the spatial scale of movement must increase.**

This predicts a continuum:

```
local environmental change
        ↓
within-home-range state buffering
        ↓
local buffering capacity exceeded
        ↓
home-range shift / broader relocation
```

The independent South Carolina King Rail study is currently a boundary example of the broader-relocation regime, not a replication of the Lake Erie coupling metric.

---

## 6. What would make the paper substantially broader

The highest-value external test is not another species showing reduced variance.

It is one of the following:

### A. Same estimand, independent population

Repeated used state + time-matched local availability in another wetland population.

This would directly replicate the environmental-transmission coefficient.

### B. Local buffering -> relocation boundary

A dataset with both:

- local availability mismatch;
- movement scale / home-range shift.

Prediction:

> stronger failure of local state compensation predicts larger spatial relocation.

### C. Dynamic local habitat surface

A repeated habitat surface allowing direct estimation of how much suitable state remained reachable inside the existing home range.

Prediction:

> buffering weakens as the locally reachable suitable-state portfolio contracts.

These are stronger generality tests than adding another Lake Erie post-hoc statistic.

---

## 7. Current novelty verdict

### Strong

- event-matched dynamic exposure counterfactual;
- direct quantification of how local environmental change is transmitted into experienced state;
- individual biological replication rather than treating points as independent;
- evidence that buffering remains within flooded habitat and persists through many extreme-mismatch events.

### Moderate / already established conceptually

- habitat selection reduces environmental variance;
- movement can regulate experienced environments;
- environmental/niche tracking;
- residence does not imply environmental passivity.

### Unresolved

- measured geographic displacement causing the buffering;
- local portfolio size causing retention;
- threshold from local buffering to relocation;
- taxonomic generality.

---

## Paper-level framing

Preferred ecological question:

> **How much of local environmental change must a resident animal actually experience?**

Preferred answer:

> **Much less than is locally available: King Rails transmitted only a small fraction of matched local water-depth variation into their realised used state.**

That is the sharpest distinction from ordinary habitat-selection and variance-reduction studies.
