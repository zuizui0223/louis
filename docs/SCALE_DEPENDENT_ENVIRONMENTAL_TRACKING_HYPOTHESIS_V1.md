# Scale-dependent environmental tracking hypothesis

## Current evidence

The Lake Erie King Rail result shows strong within-home-range hydrological buffering:
realised used water depth varies much less through time than event-matched local availability.

The broader movement literature and the open Senegal Delta godwit system show that this is not a universal rule.
Black-tailed Godwits in the Senegal Delta shift both geographic position and broad habitat use across the nonbreeding season, moving between rice-field and natural-wetland states as seasonal resources change.

Thus the useful general question is not:

> do animals preserve one environmental state while moving?

It is:

> **when does movement buffer environmental change, and when does it track a changing resource by changing environmental state as well?**

## Scale-dependent hypothesis

We distinguish two regimes.

### Regime 1 — local environmental buffering

When suitable states remain available within a familiar local landscape, individuals can redistribute among nearby patches so that environmental change in local availability is only weakly transmitted into experienced conditions.

Lake Erie King Rails are consistent with this regime.

### Regime 2 — state replacement / broader relocation

When local suitable states disappear, become inaccessible, or change in ecological value, animals may increase movement scale and accept a different environmental state.

The Senegal Delta godwit system is consistent with seasonal habitat-state replacement rather than strict state fidelity.
The South Carolina King Rail study provides a same-species boundary example in which some individuals shift seasonal home ranges by kilometres when managed wetland conditions change.

## General prediction

> **The spatial scale of movement should increase as the local availability of the preferred environmental state declines; whether experienced state is conserved depends on whether an equivalent state remains reachable.**

This predicts a transition from:
local patch switching -> broader relocation -> environmental-state replacement.

## What would make this a general movement-ecology result

A decisive test needs repeated, time-matched availability at more than one spatial scale.

For each individual and time step estimate:

1. local availability around the current home range;
2. experienced environmental state;
3. movement distance / relocation scale;
4. whether a comparable environmental state remains available locally.

The key interaction is:

```
movement scale
  ~ local state mismatch
  * local availability of preferred state
```

with a second endpoint asking whether experienced environmental-state displacement rises once local compensation becomes impossible.

## Boundary

The current King Rail data establish local buffering but do not measure the threshold at which broader relocation begins.
The godwit comparison is an external contrast, not a direct replication of the Lake Erie matched-availability estimand.
