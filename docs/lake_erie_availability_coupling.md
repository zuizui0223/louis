# Lake Erie King Rail: hydrological availability coupling

## Question

The State Retention Index showed that King Rails experience a much smoother water-depth trajectory than event-matched local availability.

A stricter ecological question is:

> **when local available water depth changes, how much does the water depth actually used by the bird change with it?**

This distinguishes simple passive exposure from hydrological-state buffering.

## Metric

For each matched event:

- A_t = mean water depth of the local random plots;
- U_t = water depth at the used/homing plot.

Within each bird, estimate the slope of U_t on A_t.

A pseudo-used null is generated by choosing one of the event-matched random plots at every event and fitting the same slope. Because pseudo-used depth comes from the same local availability set, its expected slope is approximately 1.

Interpretation:

- slope near 1: used state tracks local availability almost passively;
- slope far below 1: experienced water depth is buffered against local availability change;
- slope near 0: near-complete stabilization of experienced water depth.

## Primary result

Across 190 matched events from 10 birds:

- pooled within-bird observed slope: **0.162**
- pseudo-used null median slope: **1.001**
- coupling reduction: **83.8%**
- Monte Carlo lower-tail p: **< 5e-5**

At the individual level:

- median observed slope: **0.122**
- median coupling reduction: **87.8%**
- **10/10 birds** had slopes below their own pseudo-null median
- **10/10 birds** had individual Monte Carlo p < 0.05

Thus local habitat could become deeper or shallower while the water depth actually used by a bird changed only weakly.

## Flooded-habitat robustness

### Remove dry random plots

182 events remain:

- pooled slope: **0.145**
- null median: **1.000**
- coupling reduction: **85.5%**
- 10/10 birds below their null median.

### Require every retained point to be flooded

131 events remain:

- pooled slope: **0.180**
- null median: **1.001**
- coupling reduction: **82.0%**
- 10/10 birds below their null median.

The result therefore cannot be reduced to birds selecting water instead of dry ground.

## Biological interpretation

The source habitat-selection result asks which microhabitats are used more than available.

The present result asks how much temporal variation in nearby hydrology is transmitted into the environment actually experienced by an individual.

> **Only about 16% of local water-depth variation is transmitted into used water depth under the pooled within-bird slope, relative to an event-matched pseudo-used expectation near 100%.**

Equivalently, realised habitat use dampens the local hydrological signal by about **84%**.

This provides a mechanism-level decomposition of the earlier SRI result and supports individual hydrological-state buffering / within-home-range micro-niche regulation.

## What remains unresolved

The public coordinate files still lack a verified event/date key.

Therefore this result does not establish that a particular geographic displacement causes the buffering.

The supported claim is:

> **King Rails strongly decouple the hydrological state they experience from temporal changes in nearby available habitat.**

The explicit geographic movement-mediated mechanism remains one level stronger.

## Evidence boundary

This availability-coupling analysis was developed after the primary SRI result. It is a mechanistic decomposition and robustness analysis of the same independent Lake Erie dataset, not a second independent replication.
