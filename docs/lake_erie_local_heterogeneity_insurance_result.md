# Lake Erie local hydrological heterogeneity as buffering opportunity

## Question

The primary King Rail result shows that experienced water depth varies far less than local time-matched availability. A stronger mechanism question is:

> **Does local hydrological heterogeneity itself provide the opportunity for that buffering?**

At each matched event with two retained random plots:

- mean random water depth defines the local mean state;
- the range between the two random depths is a sparse measure of local hydrological heterogeneity;
- the used water depth is compared with the bird's own typical used state.

The primary model asks whether, after the degree of local mean-state mismatch is represented, greater local heterogeneity is associated with less displacement of the water depth actually used.

## Result

Eligible data:

- **173** matched events;
- **10** birds.

Observed within-bird regression:

- mismatch coefficient: **0.1063**;
- local-heterogeneity coefficient: **−0.0431**.

Matched pseudo-use null:

- median heterogeneity coefficient: **+0.2617**;
- lower-tail Monte Carlo **p = 0.000020** (50,000 replicates).

Thus the observed relationship is not what would arise by simply drawing one of the local random states. Under pseudo-use, broader local availability tends to increase state displacement; in the realised used states, the coefficient is slightly negative.

Supported interpretation:

> **fine-scale hydrological heterogeneity is compatible with providing a local portfolio of states from which King Rails can maintain a relatively stable experienced water-depth state.**

## What did not pass

A stronger secondary prediction asked whether heterogeneity becomes especially valuable as mean local hydrology becomes more unusual.

The mismatch × heterogeneity coefficient was **−0.00490**, but the matched-null lower-tail probability was **p = 0.753**.

Therefore do **not** claim:

> heterogeneity becomes disproportionately more protective under extreme hydrological mismatch.

The supported result is the simpler main effect relative to matched pseudo-use.

## Ecological significance

This changes the interpretation of wetland heterogeneity.

A heterogeneous home range is not only a collection of habitat types or a diversity statistic. It can act as a **state portfolio**: when local hydrology changes through time, simultaneous shallow/deep alternatives can allow an animal to keep the environment it actually experiences much more stable than the surrounding mean state.

That gives a concrete mechanism linking:

```
local environmental heterogeneity
    -> more alternative hydrological states
    -> realised microhabitat choice
    -> buffered experienced environment
```

The final causal arrow from measured geographic movement remains unresolved because the public coordinate files still lack an authoritative event-level join.

## Evidence boundary

This analysis is post-hoc mechanism decomposition of the same Lake Erie dataset, not a second independent replication.

Two random points per event are also a sparse sample of the full local hydrological distribution. The result therefore supports **behaviorally accessible heterogeneity as a mechanism candidate**, not a precise estimate of the amount of heterogeneity required for buffering.

The next independent prediction is scale based:

> when the preferred environmental state is no longer available within the local state portfolio, movement should expand to a larger spatial scale or switch to a different resource state.

The California waterfowl and Senegal Godwit systems remain external falsification/generalization routes for that transition.
