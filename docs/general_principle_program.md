# General ecological programme: environmental-state fidelity

## Publication target

This project is not a second analysis of the 2012 Louisiana King Rail occupancy study and is not primarily about monitoring error.

The biological question is:

> **Can a resident animal preserve a preferred environmental state by moving within a familiar landscape as habitat conditions change?**

## Core concept

### Environmental-state fidelity

Animals are usually described as faithful to places.

Here the ecological object is the **state experienced by the animal**:

- water depth;
- vegetation structure;
- inundation condition;
- open-water access;
- other microhabitat variables.

An animal can preserve this state in two ways:

1. **anchoring** — remain in a place where the state itself is stable;
2. **tracking** — move within a familiar area as the location of the state changes.

The King Rail system is a candidate for the second route.

## Why this is not ordinary habitat selection

Habitat selection asks:

> Which conditions are used more than available?

Environmental-state fidelity asks:

> **Across environmental change, is the sequence of conditions actually experienced by an animal more stable than the conditions locally available to it?**

That is a temporal property of movement.

## First independent test

Western Lake Erie King Rail telemetry provides event-matched used and available microhabitat measurements.

At each homing event:

- one used point was measured;
- two random points 75 m away were measured;
- measurements were taken within 72 h;
- water depth and vegetation state were recorded.

The first test therefore compares the temporal variability of used states with matched availability pseudo-trajectories.

## Predictions

### E1 — state stabilisation

Within individuals, temporal variance in used water depth should be smaller than variance in matched random pseudo-trajectories.

### E2 — move in space to stay in state

Consecutive used points may be geographically separated while remaining environmentally more similar than matched available sequences.

### E3 — broad place fidelity plus fine-scale state tracking

Individuals with established home ranges should retain broad-scale residency while switching among internal microhabitats.

### E4 — heterogeneity enables state fidelity

Individuals with a wider set of locally available microhabitats should have greater capacity to preserve experienced state during hydrological change.

### E5 — portfolio failure

If future data provide full dynamic habitat surfaces, broad relocation should increase when no internal microhabitat retains the usable state.

## Stronger portfolio extension

The previous HPI/hydrological-portfolio hypothesis is retained as a later extension.

It requires a repeated spatial habitat surface and should not be inferred merely from static heterogeneity.

The immediate open-data test is **state fidelity relative to matched local availability**.

## Falsification

The programme weakens if:

- experienced state is no more stable than local availability;
- geographic movements do not preserve environmental similarity;
- individual results are inconsistent or dominated by one bird;
- static habitat quality explains the pattern without temporal state tracking.

## General ecological endpoint

> **Spatial persistence need not mean immobility. In dynamic landscapes, animals can remain resident by moving so that the environment they experience changes less than the environment around them.**
